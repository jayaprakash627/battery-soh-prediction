State of Health. Reading a battery's age from a single charge

How do you know how much life is left in a battery?

Batteries fade. A three-year-old battery might hold only three quarters of what it did when new — and nothing on the outside shows it. So how do you check?

The usual way

Run it flat and count

Fully charge the battery, then drain it at a steady rate and measure what comes out. It is exact, and it is the reason nobody does it often.

~4 hrs per battery, bike off the road

Step 4 of 4

Tested on batteries it had never seen

This is the part that decides whether any of it counts. A model can look brilliant and be useless, depending entirely on how you test it.

Why the first number is misleading

Shuffling means charge 100 is used for training and charge 101 for testing. Those two are the same battery an hour apart — nearly the same reading. The model scores well by copying a neighbour it has already seen.

Hiding a whole battery asks the real question: here is one you have never met, how healthy is it? That is the number quoted everywhere on this page.

Before you trust it

Where this stops working

Every model has an edge. Saying where it is up front is the difference between a tool and a liability.

Limits

  • —

Left out on purpose

  • How many times it has been charged. The strongest clue in the lab and useless in real life — the model would just be reading the lab's schedule. A second-hand battery does not arrive with its count written on it.
  • How much it held last time. Same problem, worse: that is the very thing we are working out.
  • Which battery it is. Eight batteries is not enough to learn a per-battery adjustment that carries over. It is exactly enough to memorise these eight.
